# Process

Theseus reached its final form through several reversals. It began as a shared sentence rendered as a house; it now places that sentence in a harbour, where its words build a live ship and replaced words assemble another. This account explains why I changed direction, why the technical stack suits the work, and where the evidence still has limits.

## Workflow and authorship

I used `CLAUDE.md` as the working agreement between me and Claude Code. I set the product boundaries, approved plans before implementation and read every proposed change to that agreement before it entered the repository. Claude Code handled most inspection, implementation and automated checking inside those limits.

Three workflow rules mattered especially. A behavioural spec must be committed before its implementation, CI must exercise both supported participation modes, and a check must observe the promised behaviour rather than a convenient proxy. The proxy rule was present when the project harness was established ([`bdeaef5`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/bdeaef5)); the first two followed when the settle-rule work exposed gaps in the commit history and CI ([`3933e0a`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/3933e0a), [`5fa620e`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/5fa620e)). The opening story shows the first rule continuing into later work: its failing specification landed before the feature ([`18d4a4b`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/18d4a4b), [`caa0ec1`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/caa0ec1)).

Another assistant supported the project in a separate conversation. I used its screenshot reviews to refine my instructions to Claude Code. It later prepared the initial implementation delivered through [PR #31](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/pull/31) and the first README draft; I edited both before committing them ([`7b60b57`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/7b60b57), [`b90a96e`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/b90a96e)).

## Why this stack

Astro server-renders the shared state, while plain form POSTs followed by redirects make every replacement work without client JavaScript. SQLite suits one small, authoritative shared artefact: each replacement, eligibility decision and version check occurs together in one transaction. Realtime only needs to carry successful server changes out to open pages, because writes already use POST. I therefore chose server-sent events instead of a bidirectional WebSocket; polling would repeatedly read SQLite and still discover changes later. A fresh SSE snapshot also lets a reconnect catch up from the database rather than from an event store ([`e4bc25d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/e4bc25d), [`4ed247a`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/4ed247a)). Three.js draws the harbour without deciding its state, within a 200 KB compressed script budget and with the sentence and forms still usable without JavaScript or WebGL ([`9630311`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/9630311)).

## Position on the readings

[r/Place](https://www.redditinc.com/blog/how-we-built-rplace) showed how a server-side time limit can regulate a shared surface, but I did not think time alone suited a ten-word sentence. Theseus also restricts the next position through move-on, discouraging immediate retaliation on one word. Clay Shirky’s [“Situated Software”](https://gwern.net/doc/technology/2004-03-30-shirky-situatedsoftware.html) supported designing for this particular room and community rather than constructing universal identity infrastructure; a browser cookie is sufficient here. Plutarch’s continuing ship frames the identity question, while Hobbes’s second ship is why removed words are not discarded: they remain as old planks and gradually form another vessel.

## Why the product changed course

The participation rule was the first redesign. “One contribution ever” gave an action weight but left returning visitors unable to act. The simulation showed that the alternative of one standing word per person froze a four-person group 85% of the time. Move-on alone reduced retaliatory replacements from 27% to about 1% but did not reduce that deadlock. Combining move-on with settling lowered the frozen share to 36%; in the 12-person rooms, another person’s replacement accounted for 67–74% of regained eligibility, compared with 0% for a time limit with no standing condition ([`fb02d04`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/fb02d04), [decision 0003](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/blob/main/docs/decisions/0003-settle-and-kept-ships.md)). The exhibition setting later led me to reduce the production settling time from 300 seconds to 20: long enough for a change to hold, but short enough for an exchange at a shared laptop ([decision 0005](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/blob/main/docs/decisions/0005-the-ship.md#participation)). The simulations used five-minute settling; I did not rerun them at 20 seconds, so these figures compare rule designs rather than describe the deployed setting.

The object changed after three classmates tried the house version. They could not quickly tell what to change, what the two structures represented or how a word related to a part. More instructions would have preserved an object that still required explanation. A ship makes replacement its subject: the current vessel continues while old planks assemble the second one. I documented that break from the house before building the new body ([`b82f474`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/b82f474)).

The harbour also reversed [decision 0001](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/blob/main/docs/decisions/0001-live-present-frozen-past.md)’s rejection of visible presence. The final brief asks for the application to become more interesting when people use it together. Each open view now draws one anonymous shipwright, making a room’s activity visible without names, persistent identities or a number on the page. The total exists only in server memory and the scene draws no more than eight figures ([`a9c1afb`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/a9c1afb)). The later [log](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/blob/main/docs/decisions/0006-the-log.md) and [opening story](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/blob/main/docs/decisions/0007-the-opening-story.md) serve different gaps: one records material changes without people or times, while the other introduces Plutarch’s and Hobbes’s ships to a first visitor.

## What the checks actually proved

The zoom controls worked on my Mac but vanished in CI. The page was correct: the Linux browser had not been launched with the pointer capability used by its desktop viewport. The harness now gives desktop runs a mouse and phone runs touch at browser launch ([`55a6259`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/55a6259)).

A classmate found the reverse problem: “Back to the harbour” existed, yet did not restore the camera. The old check proved only that the button was present. The replacement performs real orbit and zoom input, then reads the camera through the existing part labels projected over the ship’s geometry—no test-only state. It also renames the control “Reset view” and verifies that the view returns and stays fixed ([`ee61553`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/ee61553), [`93e8bd5`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/93e8bd5)).

A kept-ship browser check first appeared intermittent. I reproduced it by completing ship 1, then filling ship 2 until one gap remained. The check selects that last gap; replacing it completes ship 2, so the slipway correctly advances to ship 3, where the same position is empty again. The application follows the rule; the check was wrong to assume that the ship it began reading would remain displayed. It now opens the just-completed ship after filling its last gap ([`db1eaa8`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-baizhenh884-dev/commit/db1eaa8)).

In the last fortnight, classmates, CI and direct browser use each overturned something a green run had allowed me to believe. My main lesson is that evidence is only as strong as the claim it actually observes. When a test, a visitor and a decision record disagree, the disagreement belongs in the process rather than being edited away.
