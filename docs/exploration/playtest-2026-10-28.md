# Playtest, 28 October 2026

Written up on 30 October from memory. Informal: three classmates tried the
deployed site casually on laptops while I watched and answered questions.
No notes or recordings were taken at the time.

## Setup
- Who: 3 classmates, none of whom had used the site before. No names recorded.
- Devices: laptops.
- I was beside them and answered their questions as they came up.

## What I saw
- At first, none of them knew where or how to change anything: they couldn't
  tell whether to act on the left half or the right half.
- On arriving, they didn't understand what the page was or what they were
  supposed to be doing. They asked me "how do I play?"
- Those who worked out how to play then wanted to know which word
  corresponds to which part of the house.
- They also wanted a preview: to see what a word would turn its part into
  before submitting it.
- They made a quick change or two and left. Nobody said they were bored, but
  the same classmates spent noticeably longer on other students' projects
  during the same session.

## What it suggested
- The page doesn't explain itself: the two halves, and where to act, were unclear.
- The link between a word and its part was invisible, and there was no way
  to anticipate a change.
- Once the first change was made, nothing drew them back: "boring" is my
  reading of how short their visits were, not something they said.

## Limits
- Written two days later from memory.
- Casual and small: three people, no set task, and my answers to their
  questions changed what they did next.
- The comparison with other projects is my impression of time spent, not
  a measurement.
